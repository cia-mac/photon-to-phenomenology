OPPONENT PROCESS · NEGATIVE AFTERIMAGE

The color your eyes invent when the signal is removed

Pick an adapting color and press Start. Keep your eyes locked on the small cross, do not move them, until the field goes neutral gray, then keep staring: you will see the shape return as a ghost in its opposite color, painted by your own visual system in a field where no such color exists.

Opponent channels  ·  red–green  ·  blue–yellow  ·  light–dark

The color on the neutral field is in none of the pixels; sample the screen and it is uniform gray. Your eyes make it. The predicted hue lives only as text and as the reference swatch outside the stage; it is never painted inside the field.

Palmer, Vision Science: Photons to Phenomenology (MIT Press, 1999), §3.2, opponent-process theory (Hering): red–green, blue–yellow, light–dark channels adapt, so a neutral field after prolonged fixation reads in the complementary hue. Nuance: a saturated red also modulates the blue–yellow channel, so its afterimage is typically blue-green/cyan rather than a pure green. The neutral test field is a spectrally near-flat gray (not the warm cream) so it does not bias warm-vs-cool afterimages.
